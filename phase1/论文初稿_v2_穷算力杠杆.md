# What Is the Lever in Poor-Compute LLM ML-Engineering Search? Evaluation Quality, Not Cheap Critics or Multi-Fidelity Selection

*(Draft v2 — 2026-07-12. Extends v1 "Cheap Critics for Expensive Search" with the online-veto result (§5.5), the multi-fidelity headroom probe (§6), and the evaluation-quality lever (§4, from T0/T1). Honest-measurement empirical paper. Numbers from `aira-dojo/` T0/T1 runs and `aira-dojo/phase1/`; data/scripts in Appendix.)*

---

## Abstract

LLM agents that write code to solve ML-engineering tasks (MLE-bench, AIDE, aira-dojo, SELA) search over candidate solutions, and the dominant cost is **evaluation**: every candidate is fully trained and officially graded. Under **poor compute** (a single commodity GPU; labels scarce because one label = one full training run), what actually *helps* the search? We answer with controlled experiments on real LLM-generated, externally-graded solutions across tabular MLE-bench tasks, and report a deliberately honest map of one lever and two non-levers.

- **The lever — evaluation quality.** Self-reported validation scores explain only ~14% of the variance in the true grade on an overfit-prone task (R²=0.14, n=71), and search that evaluates candidates at **full fidelity** deploys markedly better solutions than any cheap-proxy evaluation (true-val 0.803 vs ~0.785; regret 0.009 vs 0.025–0.032). Crucially, **which cheap selection statistic you use — single, mean, or variance-penalized — does not matter** (all ~0.78, within the noise band). The lever is *evaluation fidelity/consistency*, not the selection rule.

- **Non-lever I — cheap learned critics.** A frozen-LLM linear **probe** predicts same-task grade and, under a self-report ablation, carries grade signal *independent of* the cheap self-report (Spearman +0.36 intra / +0.18 cross-task). Yet this rank skill **does not buy budget savings** (the free self-report proxy matches/beats it — *rank ≠ regret*); its value is confined to **detecting where the self-report is wrong** (pairwise 0.59 vs 0% by construction). When we then take the paper's own future-work suggestion and wire that failure-detection signal into deployment **as a veto**, it **does not convert to a robust benefit**: the gain is a random-candidate-pool artifact (Δregret +0.18) that **vanishes on real search trees** (hard veto −0.001; soft blend +0.007, 95% CI [−0.003, +0.018], not significant). Reasoning-first critics do not help either, even with a 14B teacher.

- **Non-lever II — multi-fidelity selection.** Multi-fidelity search (cheap low-fidelity screening + promotion) has **headroom only on dispersed-grade tasks** (tps: +0.044) — which under poor compute are exactly the ones too expensive to search (3.6M-row training) — while the *affordable* tasks are **already near-oracle under single fidelity** (spaceship headroom +0.015 → 0).

**Two independent lines converge**: under poor compute, clean/consistent evaluation is the dominant lever, while cheap-critic guidance and multi-fidelity selection do not pay in the regime one can actually afford. This **sharpens aira-dojo's "search strategy doesn't matter" negative** and **pins the mechanism to evaluation noise** (AIRA_2). We contribute the boundary map, three reusable offline protocols (self-report ablation, best-of-K rank→regret, wrong-pair/veto rescue, multi-fidelity headroom), and two methodological pitfalls.

## 1. Introduction

Autonomous LLM "ML-engineering agents" — MLE-bench, AIDE, aira-dojo, SELA — solve Kaggle-style tasks by generating candidate code, running it, and searching over the results. The dominant cost is **evaluation**: every candidate is fully trained and officially graded. Under *poor compute* (a single commodity GPU; labels expensive and scarce because one label = one full training run + scoring), the practitioner's question is not "which clever search policy wins" — aira-dojo already reported that **search strategy barely matters** once evaluation is fixed — but the sharper, actionable one: **what actually moves the needle under poor compute, and what only looks like it does?**

We study three candidate levers, all cheap enough for a single 3090:

1. **Evaluation quality** — make each (still expensive) evaluation cleaner/more consistent, or select more robustly over noisy cheap ones.
2. **A cheap learned critic** — a *frozen* LLM's hidden features + a linear probe that predicts the grade without executing the candidate, used to spend fewer expensive evaluations or to catch bad candidates.
3. **Multi-fidelity selection** — screen many candidates with a cheap low-fidelity run, promote the promising few to full evaluation (Successive Halving / Hyperband style).

Our honest finding is **one lever, two non-levers**: (1) is the lever — but the payoff is in evaluation *fidelity/consistency*, not in the selection statistic; (2) and (3) carry real, measurable signal yet **do not convert into a search benefit in the affordable regime**. We map the boundary with controlled ablations and, at each step, apply cheap **offline falsification before expensive online commitment** — a discipline that repeatedly saved us from building machinery that the data shows would not pay.

**Contributions.**
- **§4 The lever**: evaluation noise is real and *task-dependent* (T0: R²=0.14 on an overfit-prone task); full-fidelity evaluation ≫ any cheap proxy, and the cheap *selection statistic* (single/mean/variance-penalty) is not the lever (T1).
- **§5 Non-lever I (critics)**: H1 (ablation-verified independent probe signal) → rank≠regret (no budget savings) → wrong-pair rescue (failure-detection only) → **the online veto does not convert (new)** → reasoning-first negative → two pitfalls.
- **§6 Non-lever II (multi-fidelity)**: a headroom probe showing multi-fidelity pays only on dispersed tasks that poor compute cannot afford **(new)**.
- **§7**: the convergence of the two lines and its relation to aira-dojo (negative on search) and AIRA_2 (evaluation noise as the mechanism).

We explicitly do **not** claim a state-of-the-art system. The value is an honest characterization — what helps, what doesn't, and why — with the confounds controlled.

## 2. Related Work

**Search-strategy negatives.** aira-dojo [arXiv:2507.02554] formalizes search policy × operator and reports that, with evaluation held fixed, the search *policy* contributes little — our target. AIRA_2 diagnoses the degradation as **evaluation inconsistency/noise** rather than memorization; our §4 is a poor-compute, single-GPU instantiation of that diagnosis, and §5–§6 show two "obvious fixes" (learned critic, multi-fidelity) do not rescue the affordable regime.

**Hidden-state reward models.** ELHSR / "Reward Inside the Model" [arXiv:2505.12225] learns a reward from a *frozen* LLM's hidden states via a linear head — the **same family** as our probe — but for **binary correctness** on **cheap-to-verify** reasoning, used for best-of-N reranking, not for saving a budget of *expensive* evaluations. We borrow the mechanism; our contribution is the **expensive/scarce ML-grade setting** and an **ablation-based independence** argument it never makes.

**Agent reward models.** AgentRM [arXiv:2502.18407] trains a **fully finetuned** LM + value head on **cheap-to-simulate** agent tasks via best-of-N/beam — not a frozen probe, not budget-saving, not ML-engineering.

**Proxy-guided MLE agents.** ArchPilot [arXiv:2511.03985] is closest in *goal* (reduce expensive full trainings) but its proxy is **low-fidelity execution** (one epoch, 10% data) re-weighted by ridge — *executed* signals, **no learned predictor, no hidden-state features**. I-MCTS [arXiv:2502.14693] adds a *prompted-LLM* value model to SELA, not a frozen-feature probe on generated code. The triple {frozen-probe critic + expensive ML-grade prediction + budget-saving search} is unoccupied — and §5 shows *why it stays unoccupied* in the affordable regime.

**AutoML / NAS surrogates & multi-fidelity.** "Predictor-guided search saves evaluations" is mature: NAS predictors [White et al., NeurIPS 2021], and multi-fidelity optimization (Successive Halving, Hyperband, BOHB, ASHA; MFES-HB reports 3.3–8.9× speedups). We borrow directly — and inherit the central warning that **rank correlation does not reliably predict search speedup** (rank ≠ regret; §5.2), and we add a **headroom** caveat (§6): multi-fidelity's speedups presuppose dispersed candidate quality, which the affordable poor-compute tasks lack.

## 3. Setup

**Tasks & evaluation.** MLE-bench tabular tasks run on aira-dojo with a DeepSeek backbone (OpenAI-compatible API; **the base LLM is never finetuned** — model-agnosticism is a design goal), a non-container Python interpreter, and **external pristine grading** (prepared/private split + official grader outside the agent workspace). **No LLM-as-judge anywhere.** The agent sees only scores, never labels.

**T0/T1 harness (§4).** A three-arm HCE evaluation harness varies *only* the evaluation/selection protocol — `full` (clean full-fidelity), `naive` (single cheap 10%-data proxy), `consistency` (variance-penalized cheap proxy) — with operators, backbone, budget, task set, and data splits fixed. Metrics: the deployed solution's held-out true value `sel_dval`, `regret` (vs the in-tree oracle), and `gap` (proxy fitness − true).

**Cards (§5).** Each candidate node from a real search journal becomes a *card*: what a critic may see — task, candidate **code**, cheap observations (self-reported validation `val_at_low`, runtime, error), lineage — and, held out, the **label** (official grade). A `view()`/`hidden()` split guarantees no label leakage. **289 graded cards** over three tabular tasks (spaceship-titanic 221, nomad 37, tps-may 31), labels rescaled per-task min-max to [0,1].

**Predictors (§5).** Two label-free baselines (`one_epoch`=trust self-report, `asha`=curve extrapolation) and four critics: `zeroshot` (frozen LLM, one forward), **`probe`** (frozen Qwen2.5-Coder-7B hidden features + ridge), `scalar` (7B QLoRA + head), `reasoning` (7B QLoRA SFT, analyze-then-score). Single RTX 3090, 4-bit loading, 3 seeds.

## 4. The lever: evaluation quality

### 4.1 Evaluation noise is real — and task-dependent (T0)

On spaceship-titanic (accuracy; overfit-prone), the candidate's **self-reported validation score explains only ~14% of the variance in the external true grade** (R²=0.14, r=0.37, n=71, p≈0.002); 7/71 nodes have |proxy−true| > 0.1. Cheap self-report is a genuinely noisy, partly-inconsistent view of quality. But on nomad (RMSLE + 5-fold CV; harder to game) the same relationship **reverses** (R²=0.72, n=6) — so **evaluation noise is task-dependent**: it bites where the metric is easy to overfit (classification accuracy), not universally. (The nomad n=6 is small and subject to a selection effect — only honest solutions survive as non-buggy — so we claim only "the survivors are consistent," not "nomad evaluation is clean.")

### 4.2 Full-fidelity evaluation is the big lever; the selection statistic is not (T1)

Fixing everything but the evaluation/selection protocol (5 seeds, greedy, spaceship):

| arm | deployed true-val `sel_dval` | regret | gap (proxy − true) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **full** (clean full-fidelity) | **0.803** | **0.009** | +0.018 |
| naive (single 10% proxy) | 0.784 | 0.030 | +0.077 |
| mean (λ=0, average cheap) | 0.780 | 0.032 | +0.050 |
| consistency (mean − λ·std) | 0.787 | 0.025 | −0.007 |

**Clean full-fidelity evaluation deploys the best solution** (0.803 vs ~0.785; regret one-third of the proxies'). The variance-aware *mechanism* works as intended — it kills the optimistic fluke (gap +0.077 → −0.007) — **but the net quality gain is ≈0**: `naive` ≈ `mean` ≈ `consistency` all land at 0.78–0.79, inside the cross-seed noise band. **Changing the cheap selection statistic is not the lever; evaluation fidelity is.** This both confirms aira-dojo's "search/selection barely matters" *and* names the exception: **evaluation quality**. (A small-n caution earned in the doing: an offline λ-resweep and an n=2 pilot both over-promised λ=0 at 0.79–0.805; the full 5-seed trajectory corrected it to 0.780.)

> The main line thus pivots from "variance-aware selection" to "**multi-fidelity + fixed-budget allocation**": if the lever is fidelity, the question becomes whether cheap low-fidelity screening can *approximate* full under a fixed budget — which §6 probes and finds bounded.

## 5. Non-lever I: a cheap learned critic

### 5.1 H1 — the probe predicts grade *independent of* self-report

Spearman, mean over 3 seeds × 3 tasks (289 cards), full code context:

| condition | one_epoch (self-report) | probe | scalar |
|---|---|---|---|
| intra, normal | **+0.619** | +0.381 | +0.060 |
| **intra, self-report ablated** | +0.000 | **+0.356** | −0.023 |
| loto, normal | +0.502 | +0.103 | −0.007 |
| **loto, self-report ablated** | +0.000 | **+0.179** | −0.015 |

Under the **self-report ablation** the baseline collapses to 0 but the probe barely drops (intra +0.381→+0.356; cross-task +0.103→**+0.179**, *rising*). The probe extracts grade information **from the code itself, independent of the self-report** — robust across seeds. The QLoRA `scalar` is weak-to-negative at this data scale; H1 rests entirely on the **cheap frozen probe**.

### 5.2 The rank skill does not buy budget savings (rank ≠ regret)

Fixed budget of K expensive evaluations; `regret(K)=oracle − best-recovered`:

| K=3 | critic (probe) | proxy (self-report) | random |
|---|---|---|---|
| intra tps-may (dispersed) | 0.215 | **0.000** | 0.171 |
| loto tps-may | 0.292 | **0.000** | 0.227 |

On the one task with discrimination room, the **free self-report proxy is optimal** while the probe ≈ random (and cross-task *worse* than random). On top-heavy tasks any pick is fine. **No regime makes the probe the right selector** — its +0.4 rank does not translate into savings. This is the NAS *rank ≠ regret* warning, and it explains why ArchPilot's low-fidelity proxy (≈ the self-report) is a sound choice.

### 5.3 The probe's value is self-report-failure detection, not selection

On candidate **pairs the self-report ranks wrong** (0% correct by construction; random 50%):

| task | n | wrong-pairs | probe@wrong | probe@right |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **spaceship** | 221 | 8382 | **0.59** | 0.71 |
| nomad | 37 | 204 | 0.55 | 0.68 |
| tps-may | 31 | 90 | 0.28 | 0.60 |

On the large-sample task the probe is **0.59 where the self-report is 0%** — a real, large-sample-credible independent signal: a **self-report-failure / reward-hacking detector**, not a selector. Two honest caveats: independence is **partial** (0.59 < probe@right 0.71) and **not universal** (tps-may 0.28, where the probe is intrinsically weak on only 90 pairs).

### 5.4 …but the failure-detection signal does not convert to a deployment benefit (the veto, new)

v1 proposed, as future work, wiring the wrong-pair signal into deployment **as a veto** — override the self-report's pick when the probe distrusts it — and warned (via §5.2) that the online payoff was not guaranteed. We ran it, cheaply and offline first.

**On random candidate pools it looks like a large win.** Simulating a deployment choice from a size-K random pool, a probe veto/blend cuts regret substantially (Δregret up to **+0.181** at K=20 on spaceship; a soft blend `argmax(z(self-report)+λ·z(probe))` cuts spaceship regret 0.235→0.040).

**On real search trees it vanishes.** Re-running the identical test with each candidate pool taken from **one real spaceship search run** (18 runs; correlated via improve-lineage; leave-one-run-out probe — no within-run leakage):

| test | Δregret (base − veto) | per-run tally |
|---|---|---|
| hard veto | **−0.001** | 3 win / 4 lose / 11 tie |
| soft blend (best λ) | +0.007 (regret 0.104→0.095) | — |
| **bootstrap on the +0.007** | 95% CI **[−0.003, +0.018]**, P(Δ>0)=0.90 | **not significant** |

The random-pool win is an **artifact of independent candidates**: random pools frequently pair a high-self-report/low-true node with a genuinely good one, giving the veto something to rescue; real search trees, whose candidates converge via improve-lineage, leave little — the self-report's own deployment pick is already good (11/18 ties). **The failure-detection signal (§5.3) is real but does not convert into a robust deployment benefit** — *rank ≠ regret* extends from best-of-K selection (§5.2) to the online veto. (We did not proceed to a live search-time integration: a non-significant offline effect this size would not survive it, and the discipline of offline falsification before expensive online commitment is the point.)

### 5.5 Reasoning-first does not help — even with a 14B teacher

| task | scalar | reasoning (14B teacher) |
|---|---|---|
| spaceship | +0.058 | **+0.310** |
| nomad | +0.143 | −0.444 |
| tps-may | −0.396 | −0.304 |
| **mean** | **−0.065** | −0.146 |

Reasoning-first underperforms a scalar head overall. **Teacher quality is a genuine hidden variable** (7B→14B teacher flips spaceship from −0.01 to +0.31), so a weak-teacher "reasoning fails" claim would be invalid — but the 14B gain does not generalize, and the scalar itself degrades on the larger noisier sample (QLoRA overfitting). Not worth its cost at this data scale.

### 5.6 Two methodological pitfalls

- **Prompt-truncation bug.** Code placed *before* the instruction block in a length-limited prompt → truncation dropped the "analyze-then-score" instruction → the model **continued the code** and the parser grabbed a random number; the reasoning critic *never ran* for a whole phase, undetectable from metrics alone (only raw-generation dumps revealed it).
- **Code-length confound.** Shortening code to fit the reasoning critic's VRAM **collapsed the probe's ablated Spearman from +0.41 to +0.02** — the probe reads the code. A confound in one arm silently inverted another arm's headline.

## 6. Non-lever II: multi-fidelity selection has no headroom in the affordable regime (new)

If the lever is evaluation fidelity (§4), the natural hope is **multi-fidelity**: screen many candidates cheaply, promote the promising few to full evaluation (Hyperband/ASHA). Before building the machinery, we probe whether it has **headroom** on our tasks — purely offline, from the 289 cards' free self-report + true grade. Budget model: from a candidate pool, a budget of B full-eval-equivalents; `single` = best true among B random candidates (quality unknown a priori); `mf(self-report)` = best true among the top-B by the free self-report screen; `oracle` = pool max. We compute on **real search-tree pools and random pools** (having just learned in §5.4 that random pools inflate).

| task | B | single | mf(self-report) | oracle | **headroom = mf − single** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| spaceship | 3 | 0.954 | 0.969 | 0.981 | **+0.015** |
| spaceship | 10 | 0.982 | 0.982 | 0.986 | **−0.000** |
| nomad | 3 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | **+0.000** |
| tps-may | 3 | 0.783 | 0.827 | 0.829 | **+0.044** |

(Real-tree and random-pool headroom agree here — no §5.4-style artifact.) **The affordable tasks have no headroom**: single-fidelity is already near-oracle on spaceship (0.95→0.98, oracle 0.98) and trivially solved on nomad, so multi-fidelity screening recovers at most +0.015 and nothing by B=10. **The only task with real headroom is tps-may (+0.044)** — the dispersed-grade task — where the free self-report screen nearly reaches the oracle. But the tps *family* is precisely what poor compute **cannot afford**: its full evaluation trains on millions of rows (a sibling task, tps-dec-2021, has a 3.6M-row training set; a single candidate takes 1–3 h), so a cheap wide multi-fidelity search there is infeasible on one GPU. **The tasks with multi-fidelity headroom are the ones poor compute can't search; the ones it can search are already near-oracle under single fidelity.** Multi-fidelity *selection* is not a lever in the affordable regime.

## 7. Discussion

**Two independent lines converge.** A cheap learned critic (§5) and multi-fidelity selection (§6) are methodologically unrelated, yet both fail the same way: they carry genuine signal (the probe's ablation-verified independence; the self-report's near-oracle screen on dispersed tasks) that **does not convert into a search benefit in the regime one can afford**. The critic's rank skill dies as *rank ≠ regret* across three framings (best-of-K, wrong-pair selection, online veto); multi-fidelity's speedup dies for lack of *headroom* on affordable tasks. The one thing that *does* move the deployed solution is **evaluation quality** (§4) — and even there, the payoff is fidelity/consistency, not the selection statistic.

**Relation to prior negatives.** aira-dojo found search strategy barely matters; we **sharpen** this — under poor compute it is not just the *policy* but the whole family of cheap search-side interventions (learned critics, multi-fidelity selection, selection statistics) that fail to pay — and we **pin the mechanism** to evaluation noise (AIRA_2): §4 shows the noise is real and task-dependent, and §5–§6 show that *routing around* it cheaply (predict the grade, or screen at low fidelity) does not work in the affordable regime; you have to *pay for clean evaluation*.

**A methodological through-line: offline falsification before online commitment.** Four times a cheap offline probe killed an expensive build before it started — the best-of-K sim (§5.2), the random-vs-real-tree veto (§5.4), the bootstrap significance (§5.4), and the multi-fidelity headroom (§6). Each "positive-looking" result (probe rank; random-pool veto +0.18; multi-fidelity's textbook speedups) was an artifact that a seconds-to-minutes offline test dissolved.

**Limitations.** Three tabular tasks; top-heavy grade distributions compress selection headroom (which is itself part of the finding). H1's positive rests on the frozen probe, not the QLoRA critics. The multi-fidelity probe uses the *free self-report* as the cheap rung; a *deliberate* low-fidelity execution rung (ArchPilot-style, requiring re-runs) is untested and is the natural next probe if a small dispersed task can be found. T0/T1 are single-GPU, few-seed; the eval-noise task-dependence (spaceship 0.14 vs nomad 0.72) deserves more tasks.

**Reusable artifacts.** The self-report **ablation** (independence test), the best-of-K **rank→regret** protocol, the wrong-pair/veto **rescue** metric with its **real-tree vs random-pool** control, and the multi-fidelity **headroom** probe — all offline, all cheap, all designed to falsify before you build.

---

## 8. Conclusion

Under poor compute, the lever in LLM ML-engineering search is **evaluation quality** — clean, consistent, full-fidelity grading — not the selection statistic, not a cheap learned critic, and not multi-fidelity screening. The critic carries ablation-verified signal but cannot select under budget and its failure-detection value does not survive as an online veto; multi-fidelity pays only on dispersed tasks that poor compute cannot afford. The honest map — one lever, two well-characterized non-levers, each confound controlled and each expensive build pre-empted by a cheap offline falsification — is the contribution.
